*Since Camel {since}*

*{component-header}*

The Vert.x MQTT component is an MQTT client for MQTT 3.1.1 and MQTT 5. A consumer subscribes to a topic filter, a
producer publishes on a topic. It uses the https://vertx.io/docs/vertx-mqtt/java/[Vert.x MQTT] client, which the
Vert.x project marks as a technology preview, its MQTT 5 support included.

Maven users will need to add the following dependency to their `pom.xml` for this component:

[source,xml]
----
<dependency>
    <groupId>org.apache.camel</groupId>
    <artifactId>camel-vertx-mqtt</artifactId>
    <version>x.x.x</version>
    <!-- use the same version as your Camel core version -->
</dependency>
----

== URI format

----

----

The topic of a producer names one topic, the topic filter of a consumer may hold the wildcards `+` and `#`.

== Usage

=== Broker URL

The `brokerUrl` option names the broker and how to reach it:

[width="100%",cols="1,1,3",options="header"]
|===
| Scheme | Default port | Connection
| `tcp`, `mqtt` | 1883 | plain TCP
| `ssl`, `mqtts` | 8883 | TLS
|===

User information in the URL is refused. Credentials belong into the `username` and `password` options, so that they
do not end up in logs together with the URL. The WebSocket schemes `ws` and `wss` are refused as well, until the
Vert.x MQTT client supports the WebSocket transport.

=== Payload

The consumer sets the body to the payload as a `byte[]`, the topic it arrived on is in the `CamelVertxMqttTopic`
header. The producer sends whatever the body converts to a `byte[]`: text, bytes, a stream or a file. A message
without a body is sent with an empty payload.

The headers the consumer sets, `CamelVertxMqttQos` and `CamelVertxMqttRetained` among them, describe the message
that arrived. The producer does not read them: a route that bridges one broker to another would otherwise publish
every retained message it receives as retained again. To choose the quality of service, the retained flag or the
topic of a single message, the producer reads `CamelVertxMqttOverrideQos`, `CamelVertxMqttOverrideRetained` and
`CamelVertxMqttOverrideTopic`.

=== Connection

The consumer and the producer of an endpoint share one connection. It is opened when the first of them starts and
closed when the last of them stops. Two endpoints are two connections, and since a broker allows one connection per
client identifier, two endpoints must not share a `clientId`. Without a `clientId` a connection gets a random one,
which it keeps for every reconnect. A session the broker keeps (`cleanStart=false`) is only found again after a
restart of the route with a `clientId` set.

With `automaticReconnect` (the default) a lost connection is opened again, after `reconnectDelay` and then with a
delay that doubles on every attempt, up to `maxReconnectDelay`, plus up to a fifth of it at random, so that the
clients of a broker that went away do not all come back at the same moment. Every attempt uses a new Vert.x client,
so that nothing of the lost connection carries over. If the broker cannot be reached when a route starts, the route
starts all the same and the component keeps trying. A producer waits up to `connectTimeout` for the connection before
the exchange fails. Without `automaticReconnect` a broker that cannot be reached fails the start of the route.

The Vert.x client keeps no session of its own: a message the broker did not acknowledge when the connection was lost
is not sent again, its exchange fails. A redelivery by the error handler of the route sends it as a new message, so
across a lost connection quality of service 2 gives at least once, not exactly once: the broker can receive the
message twice.

After a reconnect the exchanges that waited for the connection are sent at once. More of them than
`maxInflightQueue` fail right away, as they would while connected.

With `cleanStart=false` the broker keeps the session of the client, with MQTT 5 for `sessionExpiryInterval` seconds.
Keep `maxReconnectDelay` well below `sessionExpiryInterval`: a reconnect after a lost connection that comes later
finds no session, and the messages the broker held for it are gone.
A consumer that stops then keeps its subscription at the broker, which holds the messages of quality of service 1 and
2 until the consumer connects again with the same `clientId`. After a reconnect to a session the broker kept, the
consumer does not subscribe again: the session still holds the subscription, and subscribing again would deliver the
retained messages once more. With `cleanStart=true` (the default) a consumer subscribes after every connect, and
unsubscribes when it stops.

The keep alive of the Vert.x client only sends a PINGREQ when it wrote nothing for `keepAliveInterval` seconds. A
producer that publishes continuously therefore does not notice a broker that stopped answering, until `ackTimeout`
fails its messages of quality of service 1 and 2.

=== Quality of service and acknowledgement

The producer completes an exchange when the broker acknowledged the message, with PUBACK for quality of service 1 and
PUBCOMP for 2. A message of quality of service 0 completes when it is written. A message without an acknowledgement
within `ackTimeout` seconds fails, and so does a message beyond `maxInflightQueue` unacknowledged messages.

The producer completes the exchange on a worker thread, never on the event loop of the client: the rest of the
route, and a redelivery delay of its error handler, would otherwise block the keep alive of every connection on that
event loop.

The consumer acknowledges a message of quality of service 1 or 2 as soon as it arrives. With `manualAcksEnabled` it
acknowledges it once its exchange completed successfully. A message whose exchange failed is handled as follows:

* At quality of service 1 with a session that outlives the connection (`cleanStart=false`, and with MQTT 5 a
  `sessionExpiryInterval` above 0) and `automaticReconnect`, the consumer does not acknowledge it. It stops reading,
  lets the exchanges it already received finish and acknowledges those, then closes the connection and connects again,
  and the broker delivers the message again from the session. The route sees it again, with `CamelVertxMqttDuplicate`
  set by most brokers. Delivery is at least once, a route must tolerate duplicates. A message whose exchange fails
  every time comes back after every reconnect: the error handler of the route, a dead letter channel for example, has
  to complete it. While the failures repeat and no other exchange succeeds in between, the reconnect delay keeps
  growing up to `maxReconnectDelay`, but with MQTT 5 never beyond half of `sessionExpiryInterval`, so that the
  reconnect reaches the session before the broker lets it expire. Every such close is logged as a warning.
* Otherwise the consumer acknowledges it all the same, and it is not delivered again. Left unacknowledged it would hold
  one of the in-flight slots of the broker until the next connect, and after `receiveMaximum` such failures the broker
  would send nothing more. At quality of service 2 the Vert.x client sends the PUBREC as soon as the message arrives,
  whatever `manualAcksEnabled` says, and the broker never sends a message again after its PUBREC, so manual
  acknowledgement only holds back the PUBCOMP.

=== Back pressure

The consumer processes the exchanges in the order the messages arrive, on a Vert.x worker of one thread, never on
the event loop of the client, which also keeps the connection alive. When more than `maxPendingMessages` messages
wait for their exchange, the client stops reading from the connection until half of them are done, also across a
reconnect. The broker is then held back by TCP, for every quality of service. The bound is a soft one: messages the
client had already read when it paused still arrive, and are acknowledged on arrival unless `manualAcksEnabled` is
set. With MQTT 5 `receiveMaximum` also limits the messages of quality of service 1 and 2 the broker sends before they
are acknowledged.

The consumer and the producer of an endpoint share the connection, so while the consumer holds the client back, the
acknowledgements for the producer wait as well, and a close for a redelivery fails the messages of quality of service
1 and 2 the producer is waiting for. When the consumer of an endpoint with `cleanStart=false` stops while its producer
still uses the connection, the subscription stays at the broker, and messages that arrive meanwhile are acknowledged
and dropped. Use two endpoints when both carry load.

When the consumer stops, and during a graceful shutdown, it first stops reading, then waits for the exchanges of the
messages it already received, and only then unsubscribes and disconnects: a message acknowledged on arrival would
otherwise be lost.

=== TLS

A broker URL with `ssl` or `mqtts` connects over TLS. The `sslContextParameters` give the trust managers, the key
managers for a client certificate, the protocols and the cipher suites. Without them the trust store of the JVM is
used. The host name of the broker is always verified against its certificate, there is no option to switch the check
off or to trust every certificate.

=== Vert.x instance

The component uses the Vert.x instance set on it, or the one in the registry if there is exactly one. Otherwise it
creates one of its own, configured by `vertxOptions`, and closes it when it shuts down.

== Examples

=== Receiving messages

[tabs]
====
Java::
+
[source,java]
----
from("vertx-mqtt:sensors/+/temperature?brokerUrl=tcp://broker.example.com:1883&qos=1")
    .log("${header.CamelVertxMqttTopic}: ${body}");
----

YAML::
+
[source,yaml]
----
- from:
    uri: vertx-mqtt:sensors/+/temperature
    parameters:
      brokerUrl: tcp://broker.example.com:1883
      qos: 1
    steps:
      - log: "${header.CamelVertxMqttTopic}: ${body}"
----
====

=== Publishing messages

[source,java]
----
from("timer:status?period=60000")
    .setBody(constant("online"))
    .to("vertx-mqtt:devices/gateway-1/status?brokerUrl=ssl://broker.example.com&retained=true"
        + "&username=gateway-1&password=RAW(secret)&sslContextParameters=#brokerTls");
----

=== MQTT 5 properties

[source,java]
----
from("direct:request")
    .setHeader("CamelVertxMqttResponseTopic", constant("replies/gateway-1"))
    .setHeader("CamelVertxMqttContentType", constant("application/json"))
    .to("vertx-mqtt:requests?brokerUrl=tcp://broker.example.com");
----

=== MQTT 3.1.1

[source,java]
----
from("vertx-mqtt:legacy/#?brokerUrl=tcp://broker.example.com&mqttVersion=MQTT_3_1_1")
    .to("log:legacy");
----

== Current limitations

* No WebSocket transport (`ws`, `wss`) yet.
* No connection through a proxy.
* The client keeps no session: messages of quality of service 1 and 2 that were not acknowledged before a connection
  was lost are not sent again by the client.
* The Vert.x client does not reconnect by itself yet (https://github.com/vert-x3/vertx-mqtt/pull/197[vertx-mqtt#197]),
  the component does it.
* With a kept session, a PUBREL the broker sends again after a reconnect is not answered by the Vert.x client, so a
  message of quality of service 2 whose PUBCOMP was lost with the connection stays incomplete at the broker.
